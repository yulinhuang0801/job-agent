Outreach guide

How to land a boutique internship (search funds, small PE, boutique banks, small VC, boutique consulting), and how Job Agent helps with each step.

1. Find firms

  • Search by keyword + location + industry, e.g. "boutique investment banks NYC", "search funds healthcare". Location barely matters; many of these internships are remote. Apply → Find → Web search → Search.
  • Databases: Axial (search funds and banks) and SearchFunder (search funds hiring interns). Apply → Find → Import a list → paste what you find.
  • LinkedIn: company search with an IB / PE / VC / financial services industry filter and 1–50 employees; open "similar pages" on good firms; check where upperclassmen interned.
  • Ask around: finance clubs, fraternities and upperclassmen.

2. Narrow it down

  • Too big? More than ~30 employees on LinkedIn, or a famous bank or fund: skip it. They don't take part-time or unpaid interns.
  • Same firm? Website and LinkedIn names can differ ("Palazzo Securities LLC" vs "PALAZZO Investment Bankers"); check the logo matches.
  • Past interns: LinkedIn's "past company" filter shows whether a firm has taken interns before. A good sign, not a requirement.
  • Search funds: prefer ones that haven't closed a deal yet, and searchers who used to work where you want to work later.
  • Banks: prefer ones near you; many want interns who can come into the office.

3. Find the right person and their email

  • Email someone senior: the founder, CEO, managing director, partner, or the searcher.
  • Check the firm's Team / Leadership and Contact pages. Jobs → Research firm does this for you.
  • Only about 30% of firms publish emails. If Job Agent can't find one, use an email finder (below), or check the person's LinkedIn "Contact info".
  • Email patterns: one known address reveals the format. If jwang@firm.com is Jason Wang, Mary Smith is probably msmith@firm.com. Job Agent guesses from patterns it sees (and confirms with Hunter.io if you've added a key). If you find any one email at a firm yourself, enter it on the job card and Job Agent builds your contact's email from the same pattern.

Finding emails with Apollo.io

  1. Sign up for a free account at apollo.io (your school email works).
  2. Optional: install Apollo's Chrome extension. It shows contact info while you're on a LinkedIn profile or a company's website.
  3. In Apollo, open Search → People. Filter by Company (name or website) and Job title (Founder, Managing Director, Partner, CEO, Principal).
  4. Click Access email on the right person. The free plan has a limited number of email credits a month, so only reveal people you'll actually email. Prefer emails marked Verified.
  5. Paste the email into the contact box on that job's card in Job Agent, then write the email.
  6. Apollo has nothing? Try Hunter.io (Domain Search shows the firm's email pattern; you can add a Hunter key in Settings so Job Agent checks it automatically) or RocketReach. Still nothing: send a LinkedIn connection note (Job Agent writes one under 300 characters).

Free-plan credit limits change; check your Apollo and Hunter dashboards.

4. Reach out

  • Cold email: concise, specific to the firm, asks directly for a short call about a part-time or remote (often unpaid) internship, and attaches your resume. Make it your own; emails that look like everyone else's get ignored. Jobs → Write email → Open in Gmail or Outlook.
  • LinkedIn: InMail (with Premium) can carry the same email. Without credits, a connection request note has only 300 characters, and they'll only see your profile, so polish it first.
  • Application form: if the firm has a posting, Job Agent walks through every question with you and gives you a .txt to copy from.
  • Follow up after 7 days if there's no reply. Apply → My jobs shows who's due.
  • Volume matters: aim for 10+ firms a day. Rejection rates are high; keep finding new firms.

Resources

  • Firm databases: Axial, SearchFunder, Private Equity List, CT Acquisitions (search fund partner directory), Syracuse's New York VC & PE database.
  • Email finders: Apollo.io, Hunter.io, RocketReach.
  • Job postings: company career pages on Greenhouse, Lever and Ashby (Apply → Find → Database).

After you apply

  • Put every date on the Calendar (Apply → Calendar): deadlines, online assessments, interviews, offer deadlines. Download the .ics to see them in Google Calendar or Outlook.
  • Online assessments usually arrive by email a few days after you apply, with a deadline of about a week. Add one to the job, press Prepare, and practice in the same format: timed video answers for HireVue, LeetCode-style problems for CodeSignal and HackerRank.
  • Interviews: prepare with company prep (Prepare → Skills check) and a mock with the behavioral coach. Send a thank-you note within a day; the job card drafts it from what you talked about.
  • Networking: a 15-minute call with an alumnus before you apply is often what gets your resume read. Ask for advice, not a job; ask for a referral only after a good conversation (Apply → Network).
  • Offers: record them on the job. Asking for a few more days to decide is normal; negotiating an internship offer is reasonable only with a real reason, like a competing offer.

Work authorization for international students (F-1)

General information, not legal advice. Your school's international student office decides; at Illinois that's ISSS.

  • CPT (Curricular Practical Training) is how most F-1 undergraduates do US internships during their degree. Your school authorizes it, usually after one full academic year in status, and it needs an offer letter and often a course or registration. It doesn't need the employer to sponsor a visa.
  • OPT (Optional Practical Training) is work authorization you apply for from USCIS, usually used after graduation (STEM degrees can extend it).
  • Reading postings: "must be a US citizen" or "security clearance" rules you out. "Will not sponsor now or in the future" usually means the employer won't hire students who'll need a work visa later, even if CPT would cover the internship. Job Agent flags both on postings and in fit scores, and Find → Database can hide them.
  • Small firms (search funds, boutiques) often haven't thought about it. Mention CPT early and briefly: "I'm authorized to work through CPT; there's no cost or sponsorship needed from you."
  • Remote work from outside the US follows different rules; ask your international office before accepting.

How to get an app password
  • Gmail: turn on 2-Step Verification, then create one at myaccount.google.com/apppasswords. IMAP is on by default.
  • QQ / 163 / 126: 设置 → 账户 → 开启 IMAP/SMTP 服务 → 生成授权码, and paste the 授权码 here.
  • iCloud: account.apple.com → Sign-In and Security → App-Specific Passwords.
  • Yahoo: Account security → Generate app password.
  • Outlook / Hotmail: Microsoft no longer allows app passwords for this. Forward your job alerts to a Gmail address, or save an alert as a file and upload it in Find → Database → Data sources.

Then turn on alerts: LinkedIn (Jobs → search → Set alert), Handshake (Settings → Notification preferences → job recommendations by email), Indeed (search → Get new jobs for this search by email).

Daily matches

Each morning (from 6am, while Job Agent is running) it looks for new company job boards, refreshes postings, scores the new ones against your profile, and prepares the best few in My jobs, so they're ready when you open the app. It never applies or sends anything for you.

Cost per day: about 3 AI calls to score 30 postings, plus 1–3 per prepared match. Searches use Serper (or Tavily) credits. New boards also come free from your jobs, alert emails, the SimplifyJobs lists, Hacker News and company names.

Your answers

How answers are graded

Every answer is scored 1–4 on fixed dimensions, using written descriptions of each level and two worked examples, so the same answer gets the same grade. Stories: situation and stakes, your own actions (counts double), result (counts double), reflection, and whether it answers the question. Short answers: answers the question, specific evidence (counts double), structure, and how personal and tailored it is.

The grader has to quote your exact words for each score; the app checks every quote, and a 3 or 4 without a real quote is lowered to 2. It's still an AI judgment: if a grade looks wrong, press Too harsh or Too generous. Those votes are saved to improve the grader.

Skills check

About 5 short questions picked for your target roles and the skills you listed. Answer in your own words (English or 中文) without looking things up. It's for calibrating your profile honestly; employers never see it. Takes about 15 minutes.

Prepare for a specific company

Tell it where you're applying (e.g. Anthropic, a search fund, Goldman's summer analyst program). It looks up that company's interview process and values, then adds tailored behavioral questions to Stories and technical questions to this check.

Leave the areas blank to let it choose from your major and goals.
